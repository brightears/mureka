# Piano Arranging Craft for Public-Domain Carols (Solo Piano and Jazz Piano Trio)

Scope note: the brief is "make programmatic carol arrangements sound professional rather than melody + block chords". Cited Findings hold only what a source actually says. The bar-by-bar reharmonizations, note-name voicings, rule tables and form templates under **Inferences** are my own constructions that apply the cited principles to public-domain melodies (Gruber's "Silent Night", the "O Tannenbaum" folk tune, the "Away in a Manger" tunes). I checked them for melody/chord compatibility, but no source verified them. No copyrighted arrangement content or lyrics are reproduced.

Notation: C4 = middle C. Chords are in C major unless stated. "Melody" means the carol tune on top.

## 1. Reharmonization for carols: which moves, and how much is tasteful for background music?

### Takeaway
Professional ballad versions of carols start from the hymn's I/IV/V harmony. They then (a) turn triads into 7th/9th/6-9/add2/sus chords, (b) put ii–V (major or minor) in front of target chords (tonicization), (c) add secondary dominants and sus4→3 resolutions, and (d) use pedal points, chromatic bass/inner lines, borrowed iv/bVII and deceptive cadences at phrase ends. For background music, sources advise starting with "inside" techniques and using dense or "outside" sounds sparingly. Every chord must accommodate the melody note.

### Cited Findings
- Piano With Jonny's "Silent Night" jazz-ballad lesson (key of C) starts from the primary triads (I, IV, V). It then adds diatonic 7ths (Cmaj7, Dm7, Em7, Fmaj7, G7, Am7, Bm7b5), extensions (Cmaj7 to Cmaj9; Dm9 with a "slip note"), altered dominants, secondary dominants, major 2-5-1s into I/IV/V, and minor 2-5-1s (iiø7–V7alt–i) to tonicize ii, iii and vi. — [Piano With Jonny, Silent Night Jazz Ballad](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/silent-night-jazz-ballad/)
- Same lesson, concrete move: in measure 4, A9(sus4) is arpeggiated and then resolves to A7(b9). The 4–3 suspension delays the 3rd (D before C#), and A9sus4 → A7(b9) is said to work especially well when the target chord is minor (A7 is V of Dm). — [Piano With Jonny, Silent Night Jazz Ballad](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/silent-night-jazz-ballad/)
- Same lesson: voicings are "spread across both hands to open up the sound". — [Piano With Jonny, Silent Night Jazz Ballad](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/silent-night-jazz-ballad/)
- John Proulx's longer PWJ workshop on Silent Night follows a production sequence: melody → four-part hymn version → reharmonized lead sheet with chord extensions → swing left hand → solo fills → passing chords → intros and endings → final arrangement. — [PWJ search listing, Silent Night](https://pianowithjonny.com/music-style/holiday/page/5/)
- PWJ's "Away in a Manger" contemporary arrangement (C major) uses add2 colours: Cadd2/E voiced D–G–C–E, Fadd2 voiced C–G–C with G as the middle voice, and G7sus4 → G7 for tension and release. The 1st/2nd endings use F–G–C–F and C–F–D voicings (stacked 2nds/4ths). — [PWJ, Away in a Manger](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/away-in-a-manger/)
- PWJ also names two right-hand melody-harmonization techniques: keep the same chord under several melody notes, or harmonize individual melody notes in 6ths. They can be mixed. — [PWJ, Away in a Manger](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/away-in-a-manger/)
- A guitar analysis of "Away in a Manger" (key of D) notes that the basic chords are I–IV–V (D, G, A), and that the F#7 in measure 3 really functions as a D augmented. In other words, there is a chromatic inner-voice line (D–D#/A#) inside the "secondary dominant". — [Premier Guitar, Away in a Manger Arranged for Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/articles/Away_in_a_Manger_Arranged_for_Guitar)
- A GIA choral arrangement describes its chord progressions as "faithful to the original but with delightful deviations", a useful taste benchmark. — [GIA, Away in a Manger](https://giamusic.com/resource/away-in-a-manger-pdf-d10670)
- Learn Jazz Standards sorts techniques into "inside" (subtle; they preserve the tune's character) and "outside" (they change the character; use with discretion):
  - Inside: modulation; diatonic substitution with roots a 3rd apart (Cmaj7 ↔ Em7 or Am7); relative major/minor; secondary dominants (e.g., changing a ii-7 into a II7 for a "gospel" feel); diminished substitution (for C7: Db°, E°, G° and Bb° share 3 notes).
  - Outside: chromatic bass lines under a fixed melody; pedal tones; quartal/secundal/quintal stacks; parallel chords; borrowed chords (in major: Fm7 for Fmaj7, Dø7 for Dm7, Abmaj7, Bb).
  - Guidance: "start inside"; choose chords that contain the melody note; secundal clusters only at key moments; breaking a pedal releases tension. — [Learn Jazz Standards, 10 Reharmonization Techniques](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/jazz-reharmonization-techniques/)
- A Portuguese academic study of piano reharmonization practice found that the tritone substitution was among the most frequently used techniques, and that parallel reharmonization and harmonic substitution were prominent in piano work. — [RECIPP / IPP study](https://recipp.ipp.pt/handle/10400.22/9636)
- Hal Leonard's *Reharmonization Techniques* (Berklee, Randy Felts) lists simple substitution, diatonic approach, adding dominant and subdominant chords, harmonic displacement, and modal interchange. — [Hal Leonard listing](https://halleonard.com/product/50449496/reharmonization-techniquesreharmonization-techniques); see also [Berklee Online course](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/reharmonization-techniques)
- In a hymn/gospel arranging handout (GIA 2025, T. W. Jefferson), chord substitution means replacing a chord with another of similar function; reharmonization changes the harmonic landscape more broadly. Other named tools: pedal tones (for anticipation in intros, before cadences, at endings), quartal chords planed in parallel/contrary motion over a pedal, and meter transformation (3/4 hymn → 9/8; 4/4 → 12/8). — [Jefferson, Piano Arranging handout, GIA SMI 2025 (PDF)](https://institute.giamusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Jefferson_PianoArranging_SMI25.pdf)
- The minor line cliché: inside a minor chord, one voice descends chromatically root → maj7 → b7 → 6 (Am, AmMaj7, Am7, Am6). It can sit in the bass, inner voice or top. — [Hub Guitar, Minor Line Cliché](https://hubguitar.com/fretboard/minor-line-cliche); [PianoGroove Minor Line Cliché tutorial](https://www.pianogroove.com/?p=277); [PianoGroove community thread](https://community.pianogroove.com/t/minor7-to-minor-major7-to-minor7-reharm/8100)
- A "classic jazz deceptive cadence" cited in a published chart: Dm7 → Dbmaj7 (ii → bII maj7 instead of → I). — [Jazz Leadsheets, On Y Va (Jim Rotondi)](https://m2.jazzleadsheets.com/on-y-va.html)
- A MuseScore forum article on reharmonizing "Silent Night" exists, but it returned HTTP 403 and could not be read. — [MuseScore forum](https://musescore.org/en/node/152271)

### Inferences

**A. Melody-compatibility rules (the filter every reharm move must pass)**

These are standard jazz harmony practice, used here to turn LJS's "choose chords that contain the melody note" into code.

| Melody note's role over the candidate chord | Status |
|---|---|
| 1, 3, 5, 6/13, 7 (maj7 on maj chords, b7 on m7/dom), 9 | OK |
| 11 over minor 7 (e.g., G over Dm7) | OK; sounds lush |
| #11 over maj7 or dom7 (e.g., B over Fmaj7) | OK; lydian colour |
| 4/11 over a major or dominant chord, sustained | Avoid; use a sus4 chord instead |
| Melody = root of a maj7 chord (C over Cmaj7) | Do not voice the B a minor 9th or minor 2nd below the melody; use C6 or Cadd9 instead |
| Melody a b9 above any chord tone (half step plus octaves), except on a dom7(b9) chord | Avoid |
| b9 / #9 / b13 | Allowed only on dominant chords resolving down a 5th, and only in the 2nd or 3rd pass |

**B. Encodable reharm moves (each is a rule with a melody check)**

1. **Colour upgrade (pass 1, always safe).** C → Cadd9 / C6/9 / Cmaj9; F → Fmaj7 / Fmaj9 / Fadd2; G7 → G9sus4 (= F/G) → G13 → G7. Dm → Dm9 / Dm11.
2. **Diatonic 3rd-substitution.** Replace I with vi (Am7) or iii (Em7) when the melody note is shared; replace IV with ii (Dm7/Dm9).
3. **ii–V insertion.** For a target chord X held ≥2 bars, split the bar before X into ii7/X → V7/X. Example in C: bar before F becomes Gm7 → C7(9); bar before Dm becomes Em7b5 → A7(b9) (minor 2-5, per PWJ).
4. **Secondary dominant.** Precede ii, iii, IV, vi by their V7: A7 → Dm, B7 → Em, C7 → F, E7 → Am. A gentler variant uses V7sus4 → V7 (the PWJ A9sus4 → A7b9 move).
5. **Tritone substitution.** Replace V7 with the dominant a tritone away (G7 → Db7; C7 → Gb7), giving a chromatic bass descent (D–Db–C). Allow it only if the melody notes are 3, b7, 9, #11 or 13 of the sub. Melody notes that are the root or 5th of the original V become b5 / b9 of the sub, so they fail.
6. **Backdoor / borrowed resolution.** At a final cadence, replace V7 → I with iv6 → I (Fm6 → C) or bVII9/13 → I (Bb13 → C), or iiø7 → V7(b9) → I (Dm7b5 → G7b9 → C).
7. **Pedal point.** Hold the tonic or dominant bass (C or G) for 2–8 bars while the upper chords change. Use it in intros, before cadences and in endings (Jefferson). Break the pedal at the phrase climax for release (LJS).
8. **Line cliché (chromatic inner voice).**
   - Major: C – C+ – C6 – C7 (inner voice G – G# – A – Bb).
   - Minor: Am – AmMaj7 – Am7 – Am6 (A – G# – G – F#).
   - Put the moving voice in the tenor (around E3–A3) or alto, never above the melody.
9. **Descending bass by inversions.** Fmaj7 – Fmaj7/E – Dm7 – C, or C – G/B – Am7 – Am7/G – Fmaj7. This is the "deceptively simple" pro sound: the same chords with a moving bass line.
10. **Deceptive cadence.** At the expected final I, go to vi (Am9), bVI (Abmaj7) or the ii → bIImaj7 move (Dm7 → Dbmaj7). Then repeat the last phrase and resolve properly.
11. **Planed quartal colour over a pedal** (Jefferson), for intros and endings only. Example: stacks D–G–C and E–A–D over a C pedal.

- 5–8: Dm9 | Db9 | C6 | Gm7 – C9

- 17–20: Dm9 | G13 – G7(b9) | Am9 | Am7 (or A7sus → A7)
  - Bar 19 is a deceptive move: melody C' = b3 of Am, instead of C.
- 21–24: Fmaj9 | Bb13 (or Fm6, or Dm7b5 → G7b9) | C6/9 | C6/9

  - Bar 22 is the backdoor: melody G F D over Bb13 = 13, 5, 3; over Fm6 = 9, 1, 6.

Tag ending (append after bar 22, instead of the bar 23 resolution):
- Repeat bars 21–22 with Am9 or Abmaj7 (deceptive) in place of C.
- Repeat bars 21–22 again with Fm6.
- Resolve to C6/9 (or Cmaj9 voiced with D on top) with ritardando.
- Optional last bar: Dbmaj7(#11) → Cmaj9.

Tritone subs are rarely melody-compatible in Silent Night. G over Gb7 (bar 8), E over Db7 (bar 4) and D over Db7 (bars 18 and 22) are all b9 clashes, so bar 6 is effectively the only clean spot. This shows why the melody check must be automated, not assumed.

**D. "Away in a Manger" and "O Christmas Tree" (no bar map; melodies not verified in this session)**

Both are I–IV–V tunes in 3/4. Apply the same rule set:
- I → I add9 / I6/9.
- V7 → V9sus4 → V7.
- vi (in F major: Dm9) as a sub for I on the second phrase.
- V7/vi (in F major: A7(b13)) before Dm, matching the F#7 = "D augmented" chromatic colour that Premier Guitar found in the key of D.
- ii–V into IV (in F major: Cm7 → F7 → Bbmaj7).
- iv6 (Bbm6) or bVII9 (Eb9) before the final I.

O Christmas Tree's repeated-note melodic cells suit the "same chord under several melody notes" technique (PWJ) in pass 1, and 6ths under the melody in pass 2.

**E. How much reharm for hotel/cafe background (my calibration)**
- Pass 1: mostly diatonic colour.
- Pass 2: inside moves plus secondary dominants.
- Altered dominants (b9/b13) at most once per 4 bars.
- No chord changes faster than 2 per bar at 60–90 BPM.
- Use at most 1–2 "outside" moments per track: a tritone sub, a deceptive cadence, or a quartal intro.
- The melody is never altered except for ornaments.

### Gaps
- I could not read a full, free bar-by-bar professional reharm of Silent Night or Away in a Manger. The PWJ sheets are behind a paywall, and the MuseScore article returned 403. The worked examples above are my constructions.
- I found no source that quantifies "how much reharm is tasteful" for background or easy-listening music. Section E is a heuristic.

## 2. Solo-piano textures: LH patterns, RH techniques, register planning, voice leading

### Takeaway
The "school kid" sound comes from close block chords low in the left hand, an unharmonized single-line melody, and the same texture for every pass. Professional solo piano spreads the harmony across both hands. The LH uses open spacings (roots/5ths/10ths low, 3rds/7ths only in the tenor) and moves between several patterns (rolled 10ths, 1-5-10 broken chords, stride-ballad, pedal tones). The RH adds a harmony voice under the melody (6ths, 3rds, chord tones inside octaves), with fills in the gaps, and the texture changes from pass to pass.

### Cited Findings
- PWJ's Away in a Manger left hand:
  - Broken-chord arpeggios spanning a 10th: C–G–E (C major); first inversion E–C–G with the outer notes still a 10th apart; F–C–A; G–D–F for both G7sus4 and G7, with the 3rd omitted.
  - The left hand covers a wider range than the right hand.
  - Right-hand chords are rolled, which the lesson presents as a contemporary sound.
  - Source: [PWJ, Away in a Manger](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/away-in-a-manger/)
- PWJ jazz-ballad course outlines teach:
  - "Stride-Ballad Left Hand with 3-7 Guide Tones", then "with Rootless Voicings"
  - "Harmonizing the Bass with 10ths, 5ths & 7ths"
  - quartal voicings ("stacks of 5ths"); "Lazy 10ths" as an advanced LH
  - Workflow: start from the lead sheet, build the LH accompaniment, harmonize the melody, add fills and inner-voice movement.
  - Source: [PWJ Jazz Ballads listing p.28](https://pianowithjonny.com/music-style/jazz-ballads/page/28/); [PWJ Jazz Ballad Challenge 2](https://pianowithjonny.com/courses/the-jazz-ballad-challenge-2/)
- PianoGroove on RH octaves and chord melodies:
  - Doubling the melody in octaves adds impact and pushes it higher.
  - "Garner-style" chord melody fills the space between the melody octave with chord tones 3, 5 and 7 (altered tones over dominants).
  - Single-note melody leaves fingers free for turns, grace notes, inner voices and chromatic lines. Octave/chord melodies are fuller but limit decoration.
  - Mix single-note and octave passages for contrast.
  - Source: [PianoGroove, Right Hand Octaves & Chord Melodies](https://www.pianogroove.com/jazz-piano-lessons/octaves-chord-melodies/)
- Low interval limits: the lowest pitch at which an interval stays clear. Sweetwater's commonly cited values (lowest note of the pair):
  - m2: E2–F2
  - M3: B1–D#2
  - P5: C#1–G#1
  - m7: F1–Eb2
  - M7: F1–E2
  - Source: [Sweetwater InSync, Low Interval Limit](https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/low-interval-limit/); concept also at [Robin Hoffmann, Low Interval Limits](https://www.robin-hoffmann.com/dfsb/low-interval-limits)
- The register from about an octave below middle C to about the F above middle C is described as resonant, and close intervals (4ths, 3rds, M2s) work well there. — [Sleepfreaks, Voicing & Low Interval Limit](https://sleepfreaks-dtm.com/en/music-theory-beginner/voicing-arpeggio/)
- With a bassist present, a common jazz practice is to keep piano voicings above roughly the D below middle C (D3). — [TalkBass, 6-part voicings thread](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/6-part-voicings.1565277/)
- Arrangement exercise principle: bass open and low, harmony in the middle register, no muddy closed chords low, and the melody must project above the inner voices and bass. — [Ledutu, Create a Piano Arrangement](https://ledutu.com/en/learn/piano/checkpoint-create-a-piano-arrangement/)
- Ted Greene's solo-arranging notes:
  - Build from low roots, then inner voices, then optional inverted basses.
  - Vary register to add excitement and fight boredom.
  - Establish an intro figure and vary it in fills and interludes.
  - Source: [Ted Greene, Solo Guitar Strategies & Concepts (PDF)](https://www.tedgreene.com/images/lessons/other/SoloGuitarStrategiesConcepts-TedGreenePersonalNotes.pdf)
- Inner-voice movement is a countermelody device for embellishment. It is practised as spread voicings with 1–3 moving inner parts. A masterclass also covers inner lines moving inside LH 10ths and 13ths, with chromatic approaches. — [search summary of PianoGroove/related](https://www.pianogroove.com/?p=2374); [Mike's Masterclasses](https://mikesmasterclasses.com/?p=92627)
- Rootless LH voicings should keep all notes within about an octave in the middle register, with the top note between middle C and the C above. Lower, they sound muddy. — [The Jazz Piano Site, Rootless Voicings](https://www.thejazzpianosite.com/jazz-piano-lessons/jazz-chord-voicings/rootless-voicings/)

### Inferences

**A. Register map for the code** (combines Sweetwater limits, the D3 guideline and the middle-register advice; conservative for soft dynamics)

| Zone | Range | Allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-bass | A0–B1 | Avoid except a final low root octave |
| Bass | C2–B2 | Root, octave, 5th only. No 3rds below about C3; no 7ths below about F2 (M7 limit about F1–E2). |
| Tenor | C3–B3 | 3rds, 7ths, 10ths, guide tones, counter-lines. Keep about 3 notes max in pass 1. |
| Alto | C4–B4 | Inner voices under the melody; rootless voicings (top ≤ C5) |
| Melody | G4–C6 (sweet spot B4–A5) | Melody plus a harmony voice a 3rd or 6th below. Octave doubling down to about G4 in a fuller pass. |
| Air | C6–C7 | Sparse sparkles, echoes, final add9 chime. Low velocity. |

The minimum gap between the LH top note and the RH lowest note should be roughly a 3rd or more, to avoid hand collisions and register mud.

**B. LH pattern library** (C chord, bass root C2; transpose by chord root; shown in 3/4 eighths unless noted)

1. **Sustained open 5th / 10th.** C2 + G2 (or C2 + E3), held for the bar. Use: intro and pass 1.
2. **Rolled 10th.** C2 → G2 → E3, spread over about 60–120 ms, held. Use: ballad downbeats.
3. **1-5-10 broken chord** (PWJ). 3/4 eighths: C2 G2 E3 G2 C3 G2. 6/8: C2 G2 E3 | (C3) E3 G2.
4. **Wide "nocturne" arpeggio** (≥ 2 octaves, Field/Chopin-style; see section 6). 6/8: C2 G2 E3 G3 C4 G3, or a sextuplet C2 G2 C3 E3 G3 E3. Use: pass 2 / climax, piano dynamics.
5. **1-5-9-10 arpeggio.** C2 G2 D3 E3 (D3 E3 adds the "pro" 9th rub in the tenor, legal above C3). Use: new-age / contemporary feel.
6. **Stride-ballad** (PWJ term). Beat 1: low root or root+10th (C2+E3). Beat 2 (and 3 in 3/4) or beats 2–4 (4/4): mid shell or rootless voicing (E3 B3, or E3 G3 B3 D4). Do not repeat the chord on every beat at a slow tempo. Use: jazz-ballad solo piano.
7. **Guide-tone shell.** Root + 7th (C2 B2 is a M7 at C2, near the limit, so prefer C2 + B2 only at piano dynamics) or root + 10th (C2 E3). Add the 7th in the tenor (B3).
8. **Alternating bass** (2-feel). Root on beat 1, 5th on beat 3 (4/4: C2 … G1/G2); a chord dyad in the tenor on the off-beats, optionally.
9. **Pedal point.** Hold G1/G2 (dominant) or C2 (tonic) under 2–8 bars of changing chords: tied notes, re-struck every 1–2 bars.
10. **Stepwise tenor counter-line.** The LH thumb plays a slow line (E3–F3–F#3–G3, or the line-cliché voice) while the pinky holds roots.
11. **Ostinato** (new-age). One-bar loop of open 5ths or 1-5-9 that repeats through chord changes, with only the bass note changing (cited in section 6).

**C. RH melody treatments** (escalate across passes)

1. **R1** Single-line melody, slightly detached from the chords, with ornaments (grace note from a half step below, turn on long notes).
2. **R2** Melody plus a "same-chord" harmony note held under repeated melody notes (PWJ).
3. **R3** Melody in parallel 6ths (or 3rds) below: diatonic 6ths, adjusted to chord tones at chord changes (PWJ). On a G melody over C: E below. On A over F: C below.
4. **R4** Melody plus a 2–3-note chord underneath on strong beats only (beat 1 of each bar), rolled. The melody note is the top of a spread voicing (e.g., Cadd9: C4 D4 G4 + melody E5).
5. **R5** Melody in octaves (E4/E5) with chord tones 3/5/7 inside the octave on long notes (PianoGroove "Garner-style"). Use only for the climactic pass.
6. **R6** Melody moved to the tenor (LH thumb, C3–C4). The RH plays sustained upper-structure chords above, or echoes. Use for a contrasting middle pass (a common arranger device; not sourced in this session).
7. **R7** Melody up an octave (C6 region), very sparse accompaniment, for a "music box" final half-pass.

**D. Voice-leading rules to code**
- Common tones stay, and other voices move by the smallest interval (the rootless A↔B alternation idea, section 5).
- No parallel 5ths or octaves between the melody and the bass in hymn/classical style. They are acceptable in planed jazz voicings.
- The top voice of the accompaniment must stay below the current melody note, and should not double the melody note at the unison or octave just below.
- Resolve 7ths down by step and sus4 down to the 3rd. Resolve leading-tone voices up in classical style.

### Gaps
- I found no source quantifying the timing of rolled chords. The 60–120 ms figure is my estimate; the humanization notes elsewhere should cover it.
- No source covered the "melody in tenor register" technique in this session. R6 is general practice, unsourced here.
- I did not find Levine's *Jazz Piano Book* principles online in this session (see section 7).

## 3. Counter-melodies and fills: what goes into the gaps of carol phrases?

### Takeaway
Fills belong where the melody holds or rests. Silent Night has a long held note every second bar, which is ideal for call-and-response. Fill material should be cadential harmony, inversions/arpeggios, short scale runs, or variants of an intro motif. Fills must complement and never overpower the melody: softer, lower or higher than the melody, and resolving into the next melody note.

### Cited Findings
- Find the places in the form where the melody breaks or rests, then fill them with cadential harmony, inversions and arpeggios, or scale runs. Fills should complement and not conflict with, or overpower, the lead. — [Ledutu, Create a Piano Arrangement](https://ledutu.com/en/learn/piano/checkpoint-create-a-piano-arrangement/)
- Establish a melodic or rhythmic figure in the intro and use variations of it in fills and interludes, for continuity. — [Ted Greene notes (PDF)](https://www.tedgreene.com/images/lessons/other/SoloGuitarStrategiesConcepts-TedGreenePersonalNotes.pdf)
- PWJ arrangement workflows place "solo fills", "passing chords" and "inner-voice movement" as separate steps after harmonizing the melody. — [PWJ listing](https://pianowithjonny.com/music-style/holiday/page/5/); [PWJ Jazz Ballads p.28](https://pianowithjonny.com/music-style/jazz-ballads/page/28/)
- Single-note melody passages leave fingers free for turns, grace notes, inner voices and chromatic lines (the fill material). — [PianoGroove, Octaves & Chord Melodies](https://www.pianogroove.com/jazz-piano-lessons/octaves-chord-melodies/)
- Inner-voice countermelodies are an embellishment device. One, two or three moving parts inside spread voicings are practised. — [PianoGroove ballad tutorial](https://www.pianogroove.com/?p=2374)

### Inferences

**Fill-slot detection rule**
A slot exists where a melody note lasts ≥ 1.5 beats (≥ a dotted quarter at ≤ 90 BPM), or where there is a rest of ≥ 1 beat. In Silent Night (3/4) the slots are bars 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 19–20 and 23–24 (each a dotted half). The fill occupies beats 2–3 of the slot bar and must end on or before the next melody onset.

**Fill types** (choose 1 per slot; at most every other slot in pass 1, most slots in pass 2)

1. **Echo (call-and-response).** Repeat the last 2–4 melody notes an octave lower (tenor, C3–C4) or an octave higher (C6), velocity −20.
   - Example: after bar 1 G. A G → bar 2 holds E5; play G3 A3 G3 on beats 2–3 in the LH thumb.
2. **Chord-tone descent.** From the held melody note, arpeggiate down through the current chord to the 3rd of the next chord.
   - Example: bar 4 (held E5 over G13): D5 B4 G4 → arrives near bar 5's F (7th of G or 3rd of Dm9).
3. **Scale run into the next phrase.** 4–6 diatonic eighths/sixteenths rising to a step below the next melody note.
   - Example: bar 8 (held G4 over Gm7–C9) → bar 9 (melody A4 over Fmaj7): G4 → E5 D5 C5 Bb4 → A4. This is a descending C-mixolydian run ending a step above the target.
   - Keep runs pentatonic (C D E G A) in calm styles to avoid avoid-notes.
4. **Chromatic approach.** A single grace note or a 2-note enclosure (a half step below + a step above) into the next melody note.
5. **Sus resolution in an inner voice.** Over a held melody note, an inner voice moves 4–3 or 9–8 (e.g., F4 → E4 under a held G5 on C). This is the cheapest "pro" motion, and it works even at the lowest density.
6. **Intro-motif variant** (Ted Greene). Reuse a 3–5-note motif from the intro, transposed to the current chord.
7. **Descant / counter-melody** (passes 2–3). A slow line in half notes or dotted halves, a 3rd/6th above or below the melody's register, moving contrary to the melody.
   - In Silent Night bars 9–16 (melody A A C' B A G…), a descant above, E5 → F5 → E5 → D5 → C5 (one note per bar), mostly contrary/oblique motion.
   - Velocity must be below the melody, or place the descant an octave below the melody.

**Collision rules**
- A fill never sounds simultaneously with a melody onset.
- A fill never crosses above the next melody note, unless it is an "air" echo ≥ an octave above.
- Fill velocity is 15–25 below the melody.
- Fill note count: ≤ 6 notes per slot at ≤ 90 BPM in pass 2, ≤ 3 in pass 1.

### Gaps
- I found no source with a published, notated fill vocabulary for carols specifically. The types above generalize the cited principles.

## 4. Form and energy curve for a 3–4-minute instrumental

### Takeaway
Professionals plan a form with contrast between passes:
- intro (borrowed last phrase, turnaround, pedal-point vamp, or a V-chord run)
- sparse first statement
- fuller, reharmonized second statement
- optional half-step or whole-step "arranger modulation" (prepared by the new key's V7, or by a melody leading tone)
- interlude built from intro material
- final statement or half-statement
- tag ending (repeat the last phrase with a deceptive cadence, then ritardando to a 6/9 or add9 chord)

For background use the energy peak should be modest: texture and register change more than loudness.

### Cited Findings
- Intros:
  - Borrow the last bars of the tune; for ballads, the last two bars can be enough.
  - A turnaround works as an intro (I–vi–ii–V or iii–vi–ii–V, 2 beats each).
  - Pedal-tone intros, most commonly on the tonic or the dominant.
  - Source: [TalkBass, intros and outros](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/intros-and-outros.1623275/); [PWJ, 3 must-know jazz piano intros](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/3-must-know-jazz-piano-intros/)
- PWJ's stock intros:
  - Swing: 2 bars of I–vi–ii–V over a G (dominant) pedal.
  - Ballad: a two-hand run up the keyboard over V (G13(b9)) before resolving to I; the image is labeled fermata/glissando. Said to suit almost any ballad.
  - Latin: E7–A7–D7–G7, each with a sus4 resolving on beat 3.
  - Source: [PWJ, 3 must-know jazz piano intros](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/3-must-know-jazz-piano-intros/)
- If a tune does not start on I, adjust the turnaround to resolve to the actual first chord. — [search summary, PWJ/PianoGroove intros](https://www.pianogroove.com/?p=36549)
- Stock endings (Ellington ending, Basie ending, #4 ending) can also serve as intros. — [Trumpet Herald forum](https://www.trumpetherald.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=1535924)
- Final-chorus "truck driver" modulation:
  - A direct half-step lift (no transition) is the most common type, but overused.
  - It works when the preceding energy justifies it.
  - Going down deflates energy.
  - Pivot chords or the new key's dominant smooth it. Example: from C, hold G, build A7 on it, resolve to D.
  - "Arranger modulations" are optional enhancements, as distinct from structural "composer modulations".
  - Pair a key change with other changes, e.g., the melody an octave higher.
  - Source: [Musicnotes, Guide to Modulation](https://www.musicnotes.com/now/tips/a-complete-guide-to-musical-modulation/); [Orphiq, Key changes](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-a-key-change); [TAXI forum, transposing a chorus](https://forums.taxi.com/post121750.html); [PraiseCharts, Changing Keys](https://praisecharts.com/blog/changing-keys)
- Hymn arranging (Jefferson):
  - Half-step modulation made seamless when the melody's B natural leads to C (both dominant pedal notes of E and F major).
  - Whole-step modulation via a sequential pattern.
  - Chromatic-mediant modulation (Eb → G) via an ascending 4ths pattern.
  - Pedal tones anchor intros (a 4-bar Eb drone intro continuing into the verse) and set up interludes (a 2-bar F pedal).
  - Dynamics and tempo changes guide the emotional arc.
  - Source: [Jefferson GIA 2025 handout (PDF)](https://institute.giamusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Jefferson_PianoArranging_SMI25.pdf)
- Ballad arrangements build "controlled dynamic growth" to emotional peaks while keeping warmth, with careful pedaling. — [Sheet Music Plus arrangement description](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/en/product/1-1-23908438.html)
- Ted Greene: vary the register to fight boredom; reuse the intro figure in interludes. — [Ted Greene notes (PDF)](https://www.tedgreene.com/images/lessons/other/SoloGuitarStrategiesConcepts-TedGreenePersonalNotes.pdf)

### Inferences

**Timing maths.** Silent Night is 24 bars of 3/4. At 72 BPM a bar is 2.5 s, so one pass takes 60 s. A 3:00 track is therefore about 2.5 passes plus intro, interlude and tag. At 60 BPM a pass takes 72 s; plan 2 full passes plus a half pass.

**Template A: "peaceful solo piano", about 3:05 at 72 BPM, C → D**

| Section | Bars | Length | Material | Texture (sec. 2) | Energy (1–5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4 | 10 s | Last phrase (bars 21–22) over a G pedal, ending on G9sus4 | L1 sustained 5ths + R1 single line, high register | 1 |
| Pass 1 | 24 | 60 s | Pass-1 reharm | LH pattern 2 or 3; R1/R2; fills in every other slot (echo, sus) | 2 |
| Interlude | 4 | 10 s | Intro motif over C pedal → Gm7/C → A7sus → A7 | L1; quartal stacks | 1.5 |
| Pass 2 (in D) | 24 | 60 s | Pass-2 reharm, transposed | LH pattern 4/5 or stride-ballad; R3/R4; descant in bars 9–16; fills in most slots | 3–3.5 (peak at bars 17–20) |
| Final half | bars 17–24 | 20 s | Melody up an octave (R7) or R5 octaves, then thinning | 3 → 2 | 3 → 2 |
| Tag | 6–8 | 15–20 s | Deceptive Am9 / Bbmaj7 → repeat last phrase → Fm6 → D6/9 (or C6/9), ritardando, final rolled chord with 9 on top | L2 → single sustained chord | 2 → 1 |

The interlude prepares the modulation to D: A7 is V of D, and its 5th E links upward (the "hold a note, build the new V7 on it" idea).

**Modulation choices for calm background music**
- Whole step up (C → D) via ii–V of the new key (Em7–A7sus–A7). This is gentler than a direct truck-driver half-step.
- Half step up (C → Db) only with preparation (Ab7sus → Ab7).
- Up a 4th to IV (C → F): very soft, "warmer not louder".
- Chromatic mediant (C → E or C → Ab) only once, for the final half-pass, as a "magical" moment.
- Never modulate down for the final statement (sources: deflates energy). A down-shift could still suit a deliberately "settling" coda; untested.

**Template B: "jazz ballad (straight, no swing)" 4/4 re-meter**

Meter transformation (Jefferson) applied to a 3/4 carol: either keep 3/4 as a slow jazz waltz, or re-bar the melody into 4/4 by lengthening phrase-final notes. In 4/4, the held notes become 2+ beat fill slots, which is more room for comping and answers.

Form:
1. Intro: PWJ-style V run or 2-bar turnaround over a pedal.
2. Head 1: piano melody, bass in 2-feel or pedal, brushes.
3. Head 2: reharm, plus bass moving to light quarter-note motion in the bridge-equivalent bars 9–16.
4. Half-chorus piano "solo": melody paraphrase, not bebop.
5. Final head (last 8 bars).
6. Tag: repeat the last 2 bars three times with I → iii7 → VI7 → ii7 → V7 (Em7 A7 Dm7 G7) or ii → bIImaj7, then a final I6/9 with a fermata.

**Energy levers in priority order** (for background music, use the top ones first)
1. Register (melody up an octave, or into the tenor)
2. Texture density (notes per beat in the LH)
3. Harmonic colour (pass-1 vs pass-2 reharm)
4. Fills and counter-melody
5. Modulation
6. Dynamics, with a total velocity range across the track of about 25–30 MIDI steps (e.g., 45–75)
7. Tempo: tiny rubato only, with ritardando in the last 4–8 bars

**Hard rule:** no two consecutive passes may share the same LH pattern ID and the same RH treatment ID. This directly targets the producer's "same texture repeated" complaint.

### Gaps
- I found no source specific to energy curves for background or ambient hotel music, as opposed to song arrangements. The calibration (peak at about 3.5/5, a velocity span of about 30) is my judgement.
- The "jazz deceptive tag" chord sequences in Template B are standard practice but were not verified against a specific source in this session, except for the ii → bIImaj7 cadence.

## 5. Jazz piano trio ballad arranging: bass, brushes, comping density, rootless voicings

### Takeaway
In a trio ballad, the bass takes the roots, usually in a 2-feel (half notes on beats 1 and 3, sometimes fewer notes) or with pedal points in intros. The drummer plays continuous lateral brush sweeps (hands stirring in opposite directions) for a sustained "swish". The pianist drops roots and uses rootless A/B voicings around C3–C5, alternated to minimize movement, while leaving space for the melody. Energy rises by moving the bass from pedal → 2-feel → light quarters, not by playing louder.

### Cited Findings
- Rootless voicings (bass supplies the root), ii–V–I in C. The 3rd/7th stay fixed in alterations.

  | Chord | Type A (3rd or 7th on bottom) | Type B |
  |---|---|---|
  | Dm9 | F–A–C–E (3 5 7 9) | C–E–F–A (7 9 3 5) |
  | G13 | F–A–B–E (7 9 3 13) | B–E–F–A (3 13 7 9) |
  | I | Cmaj9: E–G–B–D | C6/9: A–D–E–G |

  — [The Jazz Piano Site, Rootless Voicings](https://www.thejazzpianosite.com/jazz-piano-lessons/jazz-chord-voicings/rootless-voicings/)
- Label conflict: Jazzedge labels the G13 shapes the other way round (calls B–E–F–A "Type A"). — [Jazzedge Academy](https://jazzedge.academy/?p=4706)
- Earl MacDonald's method, as summarized: alternate A → B → A (or B → A → B) through ii–V–I so that often only the bottom or top interval moves. Dm7 → G7 changes just one note (C → B). — [Wine Physics Song blog on Earl MacDonald](https://winephysicssong.com/?p=2281); [Ledutu, Rootless voicings](https://ledutu.com/en/learn/jazz/rootless-voicings/)
- A two-feel bass line primarily uses half notes on beats 1 and 3. It sounds more open than walking and leaves space for the melody. Common shape: 2-feel on the head, walking during solos. — [PWJ, How to play a two-feel bass line on piano](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/how-to-play-a-two-feel-bass-line-on-piano/); [TalkBass, 2-feel](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/jazz-questions-2-feel.1025794/)
- Even in a 2-feel, some bars contain no half notes at all (Brian Bromberg analysis). Ballad players prefer ballads to "stay ballads" and resist double-time drift. — [Veridian E-Journal analysis (PDF)](https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/Veridian-E-Journal/article/download/75574/60883); [TalkBass, double time on ballads](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/double-time-on-ballads.1264784/)
- Very slow tempos with a 2-feel make steady time hard, so the arranger must plan for it (Berklee archive arranging note, piano–bass–drums). — [Berklee archives PDF](https://archives.berklee.edu/_flysystem/fedora/2022-10/50519-Original%20File.pdf)
- In a Paul Chambers study, 2-feel gives the band momentum, and chord roots are usually on beat 1. Duo/trio ballad examples use bass pedal points. Trio feel changes include 2-feel A sections → 4-feel bridge, and a 2-feel → backbeat pedal → open 1-feel. — [Berklee archives PDF](https://archives.berklee.edu/_flysystem/fedora/2022-10/50519-Original%20File.pdf); [Jazz Leadsheets, Pas de Duo](https://m2.jazzleadsheets.com/pas-de-duo.html); [Jazz Leadsheets, Kitch-r-s-w](https://m2.jazzleadsheets.com/kitch-r-s-w.html)
- A trio bass course covers dominant pedals (including "loose" dominant pedals) and common ballad introductions. — [Discover Double Bass course](https://courses.discoverdoublebass.com/courses/307583/lectures/4963470)
- Brushes: the key principle is a lateral, sweeping motion (elongated tone) rather than short vertical strokes. For ballads, stir the hands in opposite directions toward the inside of the drum. — [BYU Percussion, Brushes](https://percussion.byu.edu/brushes)
- Forum (anecdotal): at slow tempos the RH plays the time feel on one side of the snare while the LH makes a small circle. Doubling the LH circle speed gives a double-time feel (avoid it for calm ballads). — [DrumChat thread](https://www.drumchat.com/showthread.php?p=452559)
- Brush method books with "Jazz Ballad" and "Ballad Time" patterns exist; the content was not visible. — [Hudson Music, Phrasing Freedom with Brushes sample](https://hudsonmusic.com/wp-content/uploads/Phrasing-Freedom-with-Brushes-SAMPLE.pdf); [11 Brush Pattern Diagrams](https://qsdigitaldownloads.sellfy.store/p/11-killer-brush-pattern-diagrams/)
- A Brad Mehldau trio ballad set is described as brushes "layering a velvety texture". — [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2014/12/13/the-brad-mehldau-trio-enchanted-at-bing/)

### Inferences

**MIDI/encoding sketch for a calm trio ballad** (4/4, 60–72 BPM, straight 8ths)

Bass, by pass (velocities 55–70, legato, note lengths about 90% of the value):
- Intro: dominant pedal G1 (whole notes, or tied).
- Head 1: half notes. Root on 1; 5th, root or approach note on 3.
- Head 2: same, plus a chromatic or scale approach on beat 4& into the next root, in about 1 bar in 4.
- Peak: light quarter notes for 4–8 bars only.
- Tag: back to pedal / whole notes.

Brushes:
- Continuous sweep, modelled as a sustained noise or swish sample: 1 circle per 2 beats with the LH, a soft accented "pat" on 2 and 4 with the RH, a ride cymbal very rare.
- Volume very low, with 2–4 dB swells into phrase ends.
- Avoid the snare pat in 3/4 waltz settings; use sweeps with an accent on 1 only.

Piano comping while playing the melody:
- LH holds a rootless A or B voicing (C3–C5, top ≤ C5) struck on beat 1 and sustained.
- Re-strike only on chord changes or on the "and of 2/4" before a change.
- At most 1–2 LH attacks per bar under the melody; more only in fill slots.

**Voice-leading in Silent Night, pass 2 (C), LH rootless, bass on roots:**

| Chord | Voicing | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Dm9 | F3 A3 C4 E4 | A |
| G13 | F3 A3 B3 E4 | Thejazzpianosite's labeling; one note moves |
| C6/9 | E3 A3 D4 (or E3 G3 A3 D4) | — |
| Am9 | C4 E4 G4 B4 | Too high under a melody at about E5. Use the B-type G3 B3 C4 E4. |

The bass supplies D2, G1 (or G2), C2, A1/A2.

Melody-clash check: the RH melody must be ≥ a 2nd above the voicing's top note, and should not sit a half step above a voicing note.

**Upper-structure colour** (pass 2/3 only): over G7 in the bass, an RH triad of E major gives 13, b9 (G#/Ab) and 3. Over Dm9, F major + E. Keep these to pre-cadence beats. This is standard jazz knowledge, not sourced in this session.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative, notated ballad brush pattern online; the method books are paywalled. The brush encoding above is a reasoned approximation.
- I found no source on trio texture specifically in 3/4 (jazz-waltz ballad) for carols.
- The A/B labels conflict across sources. The code should name voicings by bottom degree ("3-bottom", "7-bottom") rather than "A/B".

## 6. Classical-style solo piano (Romantic nocturne, Satie-like, hymn 4-part) and peaceful new-age piano

### Takeaway
- **Nocturne style:** a stable LH of a low bass note plus wide arpeggios that sweep into the tenor, often > 1 octave and up to 2+ octaves, enabled by the damper pedal, under a singing, ornamented RH melody.
- **Hymn style:** four voices (SATB) with good voice leading. PWJ uses it as step 2 of an arrangement.
- **New-age:** open 5ths and LH ostinatos repeating through chord changes, add2/sus colours, non-functional (modal) harmony, rubato RH, and rolled add2 chords.

### Cited Findings
- Chopin Op. 27 No. 1:
  - The LH uses wide arpeggios "commonly found in other nocturnes", needing a wide hand to play smoothly.
  - It is described as sextuplet arpeggios with a very wide range, extending beyond two octaves by m. 13.
  - Improvements to the damper pedal made such wide, smooth figures possible.
  - Source: [Wikipedia, Nocturnes Op. 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnes,_Op._27_(Chopin)); [PTNA Piano Encyclopedia](https://enc.piano.or.jp/en/musics/23146)
- In nocturne texture, the LH is the rhythmically stable accompaniment: low pedal tones in the bass, with the rest of the harmony filled in the middle register. Haydn, Mozart and Clementi rarely spread broken-chord accompaniments beyond the hand's stretch, while Chopin did. John Field was the model (Op. 27 No. 2 has an arpeggiated LH with large leaps). — [Great Sankey High School handout (PDF)](https://files.schudio.com/great-sankey-high-school/files/documents/Chopin_Nocturne_in_E_minor.pdf); [Wikipedia, Nocturnes Op. 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnes,_Op._27_(Chopin))
- Op. 55 No. 2 has sweeping legato arpeggios from bass to tenor. Its triplet figuration is a steady counterpoint to the ornamented melody. Op. 9 No. 2 uses continuous 8th-note LH arpeggios. — [Wikipedia, Nocturnes Op. 55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnes,_Op._55_(Chopin)); [Wikipedia, Nocturnes Op. 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnes,_Op._9_(Chopin))
- Four-part hymn harmonization is an explicit stage in the PWJ Silent Night workshop. — [PWJ listing](https://pianowithjonny.com/music-style/holiday/page/5/)
- Jefferson: classical style is "balanced and structured"; quartal chords over a low C–G "ostinato-like" pedal point float well; meter transformation. — [Jefferson GIA 2025 handout (PDF)](https://institute.giamusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Jefferson_PianoArranging_SMI25.pdf)
- New-age / educational pieces:
  - Open fifths open the piece; a continuous LH ostinato runs under RH embellishments and thirds (FJH, *Fairy Lights*).
  - A LH ostinato that eventually moves to the RH near the end, with non-functional harmony giving an ambient feel (*Frost*).
  - LH ostinatos combined with chords and arpeggios (*Serenity*).
  - Source: [FJH Music](https://www.fjhmusic.com/piano/display.php?item=S4133); [Sheet Music Plus, Frost](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/en/product/frost-22733269.html); [Sheet Music Plus, Serenity](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/en/product/serenity-20472778.html)
- A stepwise descending LH ostinato (C–B–A–G) is used as an alternative to fifth-based figures (Hikaru Hayashi piece, PTNA). — [PTNA Piano Encyclopedia](https://enc.piano.or.jp/en/musics/48115)
- A user-generated (weak) description of meditative piano: an ostinato of perfect fifths C–G and F–C under a free-rubato RH, with soft suspensions. — [Suno playlist description](https://suno.com/playlist/77576f1e-7adb-4f25-8829-14943440828d) (low-quality source; consistent with the FJH description)
- Method books exist that cover new-age LH accompaniment patterns and progressions: Hal Leonard *New Age Piano: The Complete Guide*; Jerald Simon *Essential New Age Piano Exercises*. — [Hal Leonard](https://www.halleonard.com/product/117322/); [Tomlinsons listing](https://www.tomlinsons-online.com/p-29701918-essential-new-age-piano-exercises-every-piano-player-should-know.aspx); [Stretta, New Age Piano](https://www.stretta-music.net/fr/new-age-piano-nr-619702.html)
- PWJ contemporary Away in a Manger: add2 chords (Cadd2/E, Fadd2), rolled RH chords, 10th-span broken LH. — [PWJ, Away in a Manger](https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/away-in-a-manger/)

### Inferences

**Style presets for the generator**

1. **"Nocturne" (classical-romantic)**
   - LH: pattern 4. Low root on the beat; sextuplet/8th arpeggio rising 1.5–2 octaves (C2 G2 E3 G3 C4 G3), returning or re-striking each bar; sustain pedal held per harmony.
   - Harmony: hymn I/IV/V + secondary dominants + diminished 7ths as passing chords (e.g., C – C#°7 – Dm, Fm/Ab in place of F at the cadence); occasional chromatic inner line.
   - RH: single melody with classical ornaments (turn on a long note, an appoggiatura from a step above on a strong beat, a short scalar flourish in one or two fill slots); in pass 2 the melody is doubled in 3rds/6ths in places.
   - No jazz extensions beyond the dominant 9th. Avoid maj7/6/9 sonorities, which sound "jazz", not "classical".
2. **"Satie-like / gymnopédie" (Satie's works are PD; general description, unsourced here)**
   - 3/4, slow.
   - LH beat 1: single low bass note (about G1–D2).
   - LH beats 2–3: a mid-register chord (G3–D4 range) sustained, often maj7/9 colours alternating between two chords a step or 4th apart (e.g., Cmaj7 ↔ Fmaj7).
   - Very sparse RH; few or no fills; static.
3. **"Hymn 4-part"**
   - SATB: S = melody (C4–G5), A = a 3rd/6th below (G3–C5), T = C3–G4, B = E2–C4.
   - One chord per beat at cadences, one per bar elsewhere.
   - No parallel 5ths/8ves; 7ths resolve down. Use passing tones in the alto/tenor at cadences.
   - Good as a pass-1 or chorale interlude texture in the other styles too.
4. **"Peaceful new-age contemporary"**
   - LH: 1-bar ostinato of open 5th + 9th (C2 G2 D3, or C2 G2 D3 G3) repeated even when the chord changes (only the root shifts, or the pattern stays over a pedal).
   - Harmony: add2/sus2/sus4 everywhere; few dominant 7ths (use V9sus4 or IV/V instead of V7); diatonic, mostly non-functional motion between I, IV, vi and V/vii-bass inversions, e.g., in C: C(add9) – G/B – Am7 – Fmaj7(9), or for interludes Fmaj9 – C/E – Dm9 – Gsus2.
   - RH: rolled 3–4-note add2 voicings on downbeats with the melody on top; in pass 2 the ostinato moves to the RH (per *Frost*) while the LH sustains low 5ths.
   - Wide stereo/reverb (production layer).
   - Melody ornaments minimal; rubato via timing (handled elsewhere).

### Gaps
- I did not search Satie-specific texture analyses. Preset 2 is from general knowledge and should be checked against PD Gymnopédie scores (IMSLP) before encoding.
- I did not locate public, technique-level summaries of the leading contemporary solo-piano albums. Per the brief, I avoided naming or describing copyrighted works' content.

## 7. Published arranging methods, books and courses: summarized principles

### Takeaway
The online pedagogy converges on one workflow:

1. Learn the melody (in multiple keys).
2. Write a 4-part or chord-symbol lead sheet.
3. Build the LH accompaniment (shells → 10ths → stride-ballad / rootless).
4. Harmonize the melody (6ths, same-chord, chord-melody).
5. Reharmonize (inside first).
6. Add fills, inner voices and passing chords.
7. Add an intro and an ending.
8. Vary texture between passes.

### Cited Findings
- **PWJ workflows:**
  - Silent Night (Proulx): melody → hymn → reharm lead sheet → LH → fills → passing chords → intros/endings → final arrangement.
  - Danny Boy: lead sheet → LH accompaniment → harmonize melody → fills and inner voices.
  - Summertime: stylize the LH → embellish the melody → intro/outro.
  - Source: [PWJ holiday listing](https://pianowithjonny.com/music-style/holiday/page/5/); [PWJ Jazz Ballads p.28](https://pianowithjonny.com/music-style/jazz-ballads/page/28/)
- **Jefferson (GIA 2025):** know the melody well enough to play it in other keys; listen across styles; refine for playability; use dynamics and tempo changes to convey the arc. Techniques: substitution/reharm, meter transformation, pedal tones, modulations (half-step, whole-step via sequence, chromatic mediant), quartal chords over pedals. — [Jefferson handout (PDF)](https://institute.giamusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Jefferson_PianoArranging_SMI25.pdf)
- **Learn Jazz Standards:** 10 techniques split into inside and outside; start inside; let the melody decide. — [LJS](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/jazz-reharmonization-techniques/)
- **The Jazz Piano Site:** reharmonization is "chord substitution for a whole progression". The module covers passing/approach chords, tritone substitution, harmonic rhythm and ii–V substitution (topic pages exist; content not extracted). — [The Jazz Piano Site, Reharmonization](https://www.thejazzpianosite.com/?p=239)
- **Berklee / Hal Leonard *Reharmonization Techniques* (Felts):** simple substitution, diatonic approach, added dominant/subdominant chords, harmonic displacement, modal interchange. — [Hal Leonard](https://halleonard.com/product/50449496/reharmonization-techniquesreharmonization-techniques); [Berklee Online](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/reharmonization-techniques)
- **PianoGroove:** octave and chord-melody RH; minor line cliché; ballad voicings, fills and improv courses; intros and outros for vocal accompaniment. — [PianoGroove octaves](https://www.pianogroove.com/jazz-piano-lessons/octaves-chord-melodies/); [PianoGroove line cliché](https://www.pianogroove.com/?p=277); [PianoGroove intros & outros](https://www.pianogroove.com/?p=36549)
- **Ted Greene (solo guitar, transferable):** build from roots, then inner voices, then inverted basses; vary register; reuse the intro motif. — [Ted Greene PDF](https://www.tedgreene.com/images/lessons/other/SoloGuitarStrategiesConcepts-TedGreenePersonalNotes.pdf)
- **Earl MacDonald (rootless voicings, as summarized):** alternate A/B shapes for minimal motion. — [Wine Physics Song](https://winephysicssong.com/?p=2281)

### Inferences

**Pipeline mapping to code**
1. `parse_melody`
2. `hymn_harmony` (I/IV/V)
3. `reharm(level)` with the melody filter (section 1A)
4. `assign_textures(pass_idx)` choosing distinct LH and RH IDs per pass (section 2B/C)
5. `detect_fill_slots` + `insert_fills(density)` (section 3)
6. `form_builder(template)`: intro, interlude, modulation, tag (section 4)
7. `voice_leading_pass`: common-tone retention, register limits, melody-clash check
8. Performance/humanization (other notes)

**The single highest-leverage fixes for the "school kid" diagnosis**, by expected impact:
1. Open LH spacing with 10ths, and no low close 3rds.
2. A harmony voice under the melody: 6ths/3rds, the same-chord technique.
3. Fills or inner-voice sus motion in every held-note slot.
4. A different texture in each pass.
5. Pass-2 reharm.
6. An intro and a tag ending with a deceptive cadence.
7. One gentle modulation.
